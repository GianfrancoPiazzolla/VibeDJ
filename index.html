How to use

Loading tracks

Press LOAD TRACK on a deck or drag an audio file from your computer onto the deck. You can also drag files anywhere in the window: drop two and they land on decks A and B. Everything runs locally — nothing is uploaded to the internet.

Features

  • Overlaid waveforms in a single strip with a shared time axis and zoom: deck A on top, deck B on the bottom, so the two tracks can really be compared
  • Beatgrid visible on both halves: the grid of the deck you are listening to is solid, the other deck's is dashed; the marked bar is the downbeat (every 4 bars)
  • Each half shows the phase offset in ms against the beat: move the TEMPO fader until both grids stay aligned
  • Automatic BPM detection, beat loops (1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4, 8), 8 hot cues per deck (Shift+click to clear)
  • Per-deck Effects: delay, echo, flanger and reverb — toggle each one with the buttons in the FX row of the deck
  • Library (button in the top bar, shortcut F3): opens a modal where you can pick a local folder and list its audio files. Each row shows the estimated BPM, the harmonic key (Camelot, e.g. 1A) and an Energy tag (1-5); tracks already loaded onto a deck are highlighted in amber. Double-click = load the first free deck, A / B buttons = load a chosen deck, or drag a row onto a deck (with Chrome/Edge you can enter subfolders and use REFRESH to re-read the files)
  • SYNC: automatically aligns the other deck's tempo and phase
  • 3-band EQ with kill, LP/HP filters, trim, channel faders and a constant-power crossfader
  • Jog wheel: drag to scratch (while playing) or scrub (while paused)
  • Headphone monitoring (PFL) and mix recording

Keyboard shortcuts

Deck A uses the left half of the keyboard and deck B mirrors it on the right. Every control shows its key in the tooltip (hover it). Select an analog control with its key, then use the up/down arrows to adjust it.

Notes

The TEMPO fader also changes the pitch of the track (there is no key lock). Above each jog you can read the total track time, the time left, the current BPM and the harmonic key in Camelot notation (for example 1A). On the overlaid waveform strip the wheel or the +/− buttons zoom both decks together (shared time axis); clicking seeks the deck of the half you clicked (A on top, B on the bottom). Works best in Chrome/Edge. The recording is saved as a file when recording stops.

Synopsis: VibeDJ is a webapp for DJing right in the browser. Load two tracks, let it detect BPM and beatgrids, then sync and mix them with EQ, filters and per-deck effects (delay, echo, flanger, reverb), set loops and hot cues, and record your set. Everything runs locally — nothing is uploaded.
